### Tech systems pillar

1. **GPS** — "GPS satellites have no idea you exist. They never hear from your phone at all." One-way broadcast: satellites only ever transmit their own clock time; your phone hears four of them, compares the delays, and does all the math itself. Killer mid-video beat: the satellite clocks are deliberately built to run "wrong" on the ground, because time itself runs faster in orbit (relativity, ~38 µs/day — uncorrected, your position would drift ~10 km per day). Stepper: satellite broadcasts time → phone hears four → compares delays → spheres intersect → you. Ending (comment-bait): this is why GPS works in airplane mode with zero signal bars — and why "the government tracks you through GPS" has it backwards; the satellites are deaf. _Strongest hook in the backlog — leading candidate for the next tech build._
2. **UPI** — "When you pay the chai wala, no money actually moves. Not for hours." NPCI switch, banks settle in batches. Stepper: tap → NPCI → your bank → their bank → settlement. Strong India resonance + finance crossover.
3. **WhatsApp delivery** — "WhatsApp delivers 100 billion messages a day... and can't open a single one." E2E encryption as lockboxes; blue-tick journey maps to stepper.
4. **JPEG / photo compression** — "Every photo you have ever sent is mostly thrown away. Your eyes never noticed." The camera keeps what your eyes are good at (brightness detail) and discards what they're bad at (color detail); the image is carved into 8×8 blocks and each one keeps only its broad strokes; the quality slider is literally "how much to throw away". Stepper: photo → split into blocks → keep what eyes see → discard the rest → 10× smaller. Ending (comment-bait): generation loss — why a meme reposted 100 times goes crusty. Extremely animatable: one image degrading on screen is the whole show.
5. **Tap-to-pay** — "When you tap your card, your card number never leaves the card. It sends a one-time lie instead." Tokenization + cryptogram: the terminal powers the chip through the air, the chip computes a single-use code, and the store never sees your real number — a skimmer recording the tap gets nothing reusable. Stepper: tap → terminal powers card → card computes one-time code → token to bank → real number never in the store. Ending: why a refund still finds you (the token vault). Finance crossover like UPI. ⚠️ Mechanically adjacent to HMAC (below) — schedule them far apart or pick one. Also shares the induction beat with wireless charging (machines pillar) — don't run back-to-back.
6. **Cable cuts & the shark myth** (spin-off from 005's cutting-room floor) — "Everyone blamed sharks for cutting the internet. Sharks have caused almost none of it." The 150–200 faults a year, anchors and trawlers doing ~2/3 of it, Google armoring a cable against sharks anyway, cable-laying at walking pace, lying bare on the mud with no trench, repeaters every ~50km running on thousands of volts pushed out from a hut on a beach. Enough material for a full episode — only schedule it well after 005 ships.
7. **Spotify shuffle** — "Spotify made shuffle LESS random... because true randomness felt broken." Humans perceive real randomness as patterns.
8. **AI agent harness** ("Hermes and harness") — "The AI never touches your computer. It only writes text — something else does everything." Model as brain-in-a-jar; the harness is the messenger (Hermes) that carries its words out into the world as actions and carries results back. Loop maps perfectly to stepper: prompt → model writes → harness executes → result returns → repeat. Keep the word "harness" out of the hook — lead with the surprising claim, use Hermes as a mid-episode metaphor, not the title.
9. **Telemetry** — _angle TBD, too abstract as-is; needs a concrete carrier system._ Options: (a) F1 — "An F1 car streams over a million data points a second — the pit wall often knows something broke before the driver feels it." (b) Your phone — "Your apps filed hundreds of reports about you today. Here's what's in them." F1 is the more visual/animatable pick; phone angle is more relatable + privacy comment-bait.
10. **HMAC / webhook signatures** — "Anyone can send your bank a message pretending to be you. The reason they can't is a shared secret and a blender." How every app proves a message really came from who it claims — the signature on API calls, webhooks, session cookies, password-reset links. Mechanism: sender and receiver both know one secret word nobody else does. Sender mixes the secret INTO the message and blends it into a fixed-length scramble (the hash) — that scramble rides along as the signature. Receiver re-blends the same message with the same secret and checks the two scrambles match. Change one character of the message and the scramble changes completely, and you can't work backwards from a scramble to the secret. Stepper: message → mix in the shared secret → blend → send both → receiver re-blends and compares. Visual gold: the "blender" (avalanche effect — flip one letter, the whole fingerprint changes), and the forgery attempt that fails because the attacker has the message but not the secret. Killer ending (comment-bait + honesty beat): the comparison itself has to be done *slowly and evenly* — if the check bails out at the first wrong character, an attacker can time the failures and guess the signature one character at a time. Real bug, real CVEs, and the fix is a function literally called "constant-time compare". Nice tech-pillar sibling to WhatsApp — that one is about *hiding* a message, this one is about *proving* one. ⚠️ Risk: less tangible than cables/Netflix — needs a concrete carrier ("that payment webhook", "the reset link in your email") in the hook, not the word HMAC. Alternative carrier if it keeps stalling: 2FA codes ("the 6-digit code on your screen was never sent to you") — TOTP is HMAC underneath, and the phone-and-server-both-just-know framing may be the more relatable way into the same mechanism. ⚠️ Adjacent to tap-to-pay (above) — schedule far apart or pick one.

   **Hook candidates** (claim-first, per retention rules — never lead with "HMAC"):
   - "Anyone can fake a message to your bank. One shared secret is the only reason it doesn't work."
   - "That password reset link in your inbox? It's carrying a signature you can't forge — and neither can I."

### Everyday machines pillar

11. **Touchscreen** — "Your phone screen only works because your body is a bag of salt water." Capacitive grid: the screen holds a lattice of charge, your conductive finger steals a little, and the phone triangulates where. Why gloves fail, why rain causes ghost touches, and why a sausage works (real Korean winter trend — the comment-bait ending). Stepper: grid of charge → finger steals some → grid reports where → phone tracks. Animates beautifully: grid + field lines.
12. **Refrigerator** — "Your fridge heats your kitchen. That is literally its job." It can't make cold, it can only pump heat out — refrigerant as a sponge that soaks up heat inside and gets wrung out at the back coils. Stepper: absorb heat inside → compress → dump heat out the back → expand → repeat. Ending (misconception-bait): leaving the door open makes the room *hotter*, not cooler.
13. **Wireless charging** — "Your phone charges through solid plastic because the electricity never stops moving." Induction: a still magnetic field does nothing, a constantly-flipping one drags current through the phone's coil. Why alignment matters, why it makes heat, why metal cases break it. _Weakest hook of the new batch — filler slot._ ⚠️ Shares the induction beat with tap-to-pay (tech pillar) — don't run back-to-back.
