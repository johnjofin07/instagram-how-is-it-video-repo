### Tech systems pillar

1. **GPS** — "GPS satellites have no idea you exist. They never hear from your phone at all." One-way broadcast: satellites only ever transmit their own clock time; your phone hears four of them, compares the delays, and does all the math itself. Killer mid-video beat: the satellite clocks are deliberately built to run "wrong" on the ground, because time itself runs faster in orbit (relativity, ~38 µs/day — uncorrected, your position would drift ~10 km per day). Stepper: satellite broadcasts time → phone hears four → compares delays → spheres intersect → you. Ending (comment-bait): this is why GPS works in airplane mode with zero signal bars — and why "the government tracks you through GPS" has it backwards; the satellites are deaf. _Strongest hook in the backlog — leading candidate for the next tech build._
2. **Find My / AirTag network** — **SHORTLISTED CANDIDATE — not finalized.** "Your AirTag has no GPS. A stranger's phone finds it for you." The tag broadcasts a rotating Bluetooth identifier; a nearby device in the Find My network supplies its own location, encrypts it, and relays it so only the owner can read it. Stepper: tag whispers → stranger's phone hears → phone adds location → encrypts and relays → owner decrypts. Ending: neither the tag nor Apple needs to know which stranger helped. Globally relatable lost-luggage/key story, with a strong invisible-crowd visual. Keep the episode about the system, not a product review.
3. **QR codes / damage recovery** — **SHORTLISTED CANDIDATE — not finalized.** "You can rip nearly a third out of this QR code and it can still work." Error correction stores enough redundant structure to reconstruct missing data (up to about 30% at the highest correction level); the three big finder squares establish orientation, then the reader rebuilds the damaged message. Stepper: camera finds corners → straightens grid → reads blocks → repairs missing blocks → opens link. Ending: the logo pasted into the middle is often deliberate damage the code was designed to survive. Extremely visual: tear the code live, then rebuild it tile by tile.
4. **E-Ink displays** — "A Kindle can keep a page on screen after you remove the battery." E-paper uses power to move charged black and white particles inside tiny capsules, but the particles stay put once the image is formed; ambient light reflects from them like paper. Stepper: pixel capsule → electric field flips → particles move → field stops → image remains. Ending: page turns cost power; displaying the page does not. Strong physical reveal and a clean black/white animation law.
5. **WhatsApp delivery** — "WhatsApp delivers 100 billion messages a day... and can't open a single one." E2E encryption as lockboxes; blue-tick journey maps to stepper.
6. **JPEG / photo compression** — "Every photo you have ever sent is mostly thrown away. Your eyes never noticed." The camera keeps what your eyes are good at (brightness detail) and discards what they're bad at (color detail); the image is carved into 8×8 blocks and each one keeps only its broad strokes; the quality slider is literally "how much to throw away". Stepper: photo → split into blocks → keep what eyes see → discard the rest → 10× smaller. Ending (comment-bait): generation loss — why a meme reposted 100 times goes crusty. Extremely animatable: one image degrading on screen is the whole show.
7. **Tap-to-pay** — "When you tap your card, your card number never leaves the card. It sends a one-time lie instead." Tokenization + cryptogram: the terminal powers the chip through the air, the chip computes a single-use code, and the store never sees your real number — a skimmer recording the tap gets nothing reusable. Stepper: tap → terminal powers card → card computes one-time code → token to bank → real number never in the store. Ending: why a refund still finds you (the token vault). Global finance crossover. ⚠️ Mechanically adjacent to HMAC (below) — schedule them far apart or pick one. Also shares the induction beat with wireless charging (machines pillar) — don't run back-to-back.
8. **Cable cuts & the shark myth** (spin-off from 005's cutting-room floor) — "Everyone blamed sharks for cutting the internet. Sharks have caused almost none of it." The 150–200 faults a year, anchors and trawlers doing ~2/3 of it, Google armoring a cable against sharks anyway, cable-laying at walking pace, lying bare on the mud with no trench, repeaters every ~50km running on thousands of volts pushed out from a hut on a beach. Enough material for a full episode — only schedule it well after 005 ships.
9. **Spotify shuffle** — "Spotify made shuffle LESS random... because true randomness felt broken." Humans perceive real randomness as patterns.
10. **AI agent harness** ("Hermes and harness") — "The AI never touches your computer. It only writes text — something else does everything." Model as brain-in-a-jar; the harness is the messenger (Hermes) that carries its words out into the world as actions and carries results back. Loop maps perfectly to stepper: prompt → model writes → harness executes → result returns → repeat. Keep the word "harness" out of the hook — lead with the surprising claim, use Hermes as a mid-episode metaphor, not the title.
11. **Telemetry** — _angle TBD, too abstract as-is; needs a concrete carrier system._ Options: (a) F1 — "An F1 car streams over a million data points a second — the pit wall often knows something broke before the driver feels it." (b) Your phone — "Your apps filed hundreds of reports about you today. Here's what's in them." F1 is the more visual/animatable pick; phone angle is more relatable + privacy comment-bait.
12. **HMAC / webhook signatures** — "Anyone can send your bank a message pretending to be you. The reason they can't is a shared secret and a blender." How every app proves a message really came from who it claims — the signature on API calls, webhooks, session cookies, password-reset links. Mechanism: sender and receiver both know one secret word nobody else does. Sender mixes the secret INTO the message and blends it into a fixed-length scramble (the hash) — that scramble rides along as the signature. Receiver re-blends the same message with the same secret and checks the two scrambles match. Change one character of the message and the scramble changes completely, and you can't work backwards from a scramble to the secret. Stepper: message → mix in the shared secret → blend → send both → receiver re-blends and compares. Visual gold: the "blender" (avalanche effect — flip one letter, the whole fingerprint changes), and the forgery attempt that fails because the attacker has the message but not the secret. Killer ending (comment-bait + honesty beat): the comparison itself has to be done *slowly and evenly* — if the check bails out at the first wrong character, an attacker can time the failures and guess the signature one character at a time. Real bug, real CVEs, and the fix is a function literally called "constant-time compare". Nice tech-pillar sibling to WhatsApp — that one is about *hiding* a message, this one is about *proving* one. ⚠️ Risk: less tangible than cables/Netflix — needs a concrete carrier ("that payment webhook", "the reset link in your email") in the hook, not the word HMAC. Alternative carrier if it keeps stalling: 2FA codes ("the 6-digit code on your screen was never sent to you") — TOTP is HMAC underneath, and the phone-and-server-both-just-know framing may be the more relatable way into the same mechanism. ⚠️ Adjacent to tap-to-pay (above) — schedule far apart or pick one.

   **Hook candidates** (claim-first, per retention rules — never lead with "HMAC"):
   - "Anyone can fake a message to your bank. One shared secret is the only reason it doesn't work."
   - "That password reset link in your inbox? It's carrying a signature you can't forge — and neither can I."

### Everyday machines pillar

13. **Touchscreen** — "Your phone screen only works because your body is a bag of salt water." Capacitive grid: the screen holds a lattice of charge, your conductive finger steals a little, and the phone triangulates where. Why gloves fail, why rain causes ghost touches, and why a sausage works (real Korean winter trend — the comment-bait ending). Stepper: grid of charge → finger steals some → grid reports where → phone tracks. Animates beautifully: grid + field lines.
14. **Ionization smoke alarm** — "There may be radioactive metal on your ceiling — and it is there to save your life." Some smoke alarms contain a tiny, shielded americium-241 source that ionizes the air, creating a small current; smoke interrupts that current and triggers the alarm. Stepper: americium emits particles → air becomes charged → current flows → smoke breaks flow → alarm. Ending: the radiation is contained and safe in normal use; never dismantle the detector. ⚠️ Say *some* smoke alarms throughout—photoelectric alarms use a different mechanism.
15. **Induction cooktop** — **SHORTLISTED CANDIDATE — not finalized.** "On an induction stove, the stove is not the heater. The pan is." A rapidly changing magnetic field drives currents inside compatible cookware, so heat is produced in the metal itself; remove the pan and the energy transfer stops. Stepper: coil switches → magnetic field flips → current forms in pan → resistance makes heat → food cooks. Ending: the magnet test—if a magnet sticks to the base, it will usually work. Visually strong cold open: pan boils while an adjacent surface stays comparatively cool.
16. **Refrigerator** — "Your fridge heats your kitchen. That is literally its job." It can't make cold, it can only pump heat out — refrigerant as a sponge that soaks up heat inside and gets wrung out at the back coils. Stepper: absorb heat inside → compress → dump heat out the back → expand → repeat. Ending (misconception-bait): leaving the door open makes the room *hotter*, not cooler.
17. **Heat pump / home heater** — **SHORTLISTED CANDIDATE — not finalized.** "This heater can deliver three units of heat using one unit of electricity — without breaking physics." It does not create all that heat; it uses a refrigeration loop to move existing heat from outside to inside, even when the outside air feels cold. Stepper: outside coil absorbs → compressor raises pressure and temperature → indoor coil releases → refrigerant expands → repeat. Ending: an air conditioner and a heat pump are essentially the same machine running in opposite directions. ⚠️ Mechanically adjacent to refrigerator—schedule them far apart.
18. **Wireless charging** — "Your phone charges through solid plastic because the electricity never stops moving." Induction: a still magnetic field does nothing, a constantly-flipping one drags current through the phone's coil. Why alignment matters, why it makes heat, why metal cases break it. _Weakest hook of the new batch — filler slot._ ⚠️ Shares the induction beat with tap-to-pay (tech pillar) — don't run back-to-back.

### Additional candidate pool (unranked, not finalized — researched 2026-08-22)

- **Thermal receipt printer** — "The receipt printer at the checkout has no ink. The ink is already hiding in the paper." Direct-thermal printers heat microscopic spots in a chemical coating; those spots turn dark. Stepper: blank coated paper → heating-element row → selected dots heat → coating reacts → receipt appears. Live proof: scrape or warm a spare receipt and it darkens. Universal object, cheap-looking machine, excellent macro animation.
- **Traffic-light road loops** — "Many traffic lights know you're waiting because the road is a giant metal detector." A powered wire loop buried in the pavement creates a magnetic field; the conductive mass of a car changes the loop's electrical behavior and calls the signal controller. Stepper: loop energizes → car arrives → field changes → controller detects → green phase requested. Ending: why bicycles and motorcycles sometimes struggle to trigger badly tuned loops. Say *many* lights—camera and radar detection are also common.
- **Supermarket barcode price lookup** — "That barcode usually contains no price." A retail UPC/EAN normally carries a product identifier; the checkout uses it to retrieve the current description and price from the store database. Stepper: scanner reads bars/spaces → decodes identifier → database lookup → price returns → receipt. Ending: the identical packet can cost different amounts in two stores because the price lives with the retailer, not in the stripes. ⚠️ Schedule well away from QR codes.
- **GFCI/RCD home safety switch** — "The safety switch in your home watches for electricity that never comes back." It compares current leaving on the live conductor with current returning on neutral; a tiny mismatch means some may be escaping through another path, possibly a person, so it disconnects power. Stepper: current leaves → appliance uses it → current returns → mismatch appears → trip. In US GFCI systems, about a 5 mA difference can trigger shutdown in as little as 1/40 second; use the region-neutral term *safety switch* in the hook.
- **Car airbag** — "There is an igniter inside your steering wheel, waiting to save your face." Crash sensors tell an inflator to start a rapid chemical reaction that fills the bag in less than 1/20 second; vents then let it collapse as the occupant hits it. Stepper: deceleration sensed → severity calculated → igniter fires → gas inflates bag → vents absorb impact. Visceral and globally familiar, but keep the safety framing responsible: airbags supplement seatbelts.
- **Email tracking pixel** — "Opening an email can tell the sender when and roughly where you opened it." The email hides a tiny remote image; opening it requests that image from a server, which can log the time, network-derived location and device information. Stepper: email opens → invisible image requested → server receives request → metadata logged → sender dashboard updates. Privacy-heavy comment bait with a literal one-pixel reveal.
- **Bluetooth frequency hopping** — "Your Bluetooth connection can switch radio channels up to 1,600 times a second." Classic Bluetooth rapidly hops through its radio channels, and adaptive hopping avoids channels with interference so both devices keep meeting on the same changing frequencies. Stepper: crowded 2.4 GHz band → devices share hop sequence → jump together → bad channels removed → audio survives. Visually strong radio-channel dance; avoid claiming every Bluetooth mode always hops at 1,600/s.
- **Solar panels prefer the cold** — "A hot sunny day can make a solar panel less efficient." Solar cells need light, but higher cell temperatures reduce voltage more than they increase current; cold, clear sunlight can therefore be excellent operating conditions.
